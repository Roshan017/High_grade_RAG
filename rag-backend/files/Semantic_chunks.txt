Chunk 1:
Public Awareness and Vaccination Uptake of
Human Papillomavirus (HPV):
A Cross-Sectional Study of 100 Respondents
Simple Summary:
Human Papillomavirus (HPV) is a common infection associated with cervical and head
and neck cancers. This study evaluated public awareness, vaccination knowledge,
and vaccination uptake among 100 respondents. The findings revealed low levels of
awareness and vaccination uptake, particularly regarding HPV’s association with head
and neck cancer.

Chunk 2:
However, strong support for public health interventions such as NHS-
funded vaccination and awareness campaigns was observed. These results suggest
that the primary barrier to HPV prevention is insufficient awareness rather than public
resistance, highlighting the need for improved education and outreach strategies. Abstract
Human Papillomavirus (HPV) is a major etiological agent in cervical and oropha-
ryngeal cancers. Public awareness of HPV and its vaccine remains a critical de-
terminant of preventive health behaviour and vaccination uptake. This cross-sectional study evaluated awareness of HPV, awareness of HPV
vaccination, vaccination uptake, and support for public health intervention among
100 respondents.

Chunk 3:
Descriptive statistics and structured visual analyses were per-
formed to examine demographic patterns, awareness gradients, vaccination gaps,
and public support for institutional action. Overall HPV awareness was low, with only 25% of respondents reporting prior
awareness of HPV. Knowledge of HPV-associated head and neck cancer was es-
pecially limited (5%), while vaccination uptake remained low at 8%.

Chunk 4:
Despite this,
strong support for institutional intervention was observed, with 94% supporting
NHS-funded vaccination and 88% supporting public awareness campaigns. The findings suggest that the major barrier to HPV prevention in this sample
is not public resistance, but rather insufficient awareness and knowledge. These
results support the need for targeted educational campaigns and improved vaccine
access initiatives.

Chunk 6:
1. Introduction
Human Papillomavirus (HPV) is one of the most important viral risk factors associated
with several malignancies, particularly cervical cancer and a growing proportion of
head and neck cancers. Although HPV vaccination offers strong preventive benefit,
uptake remains dependent on public awareness, understanding of disease risk, and
institutional accessibility.

Chunk 7:
Awareness of HPV alone does not imply adequate knowledge of its consequences. Many individuals may have heard of HPV without understanding its oncogenic role,
routes of transmission, or the preventive value of vaccination. This distinction between
general awareness and meaningful knowledge is especially important in public health
research. The present study was undertaken to evaluate public awareness of HPV, aware-
ness of HPV vaccination, vaccination uptake, and support for institutional intervention
among 100 respondents.

Chunk 8:
In addition to descriptive statistics, the study also examines
knowledge decline, vaccination gaps, and readiness for public health action. 2. Methods
2.1 Study Design
A cross-sectional survey design was used to assess public awareness and preventive
attitudes related to HPV.

Chunk 28:
4. Discussion
The present study demonstrates that public awareness of HPV remains limited in this
sample, particularly when awareness is assessed beyond simple name recognition. Only one quarter of respondents reported prior awareness of HPV, and awareness
declined substantially as questions became more clinically specific.

Chunk 29:
A key finding of this study is the pronounced collapse in knowledge specificity. Although 25% of respondents had heard of HPV, only 5% recognised its association
with head and neck cancer. This indicates that even among those with some baseline
awareness, understanding of HPV’s broader clinical significance remains poor.

Chunk 30:
Vaccination awareness and uptake were also limited. Although 19% of respondents
were aware that the HPV vaccine can prevent cervical cancer, only 8% had been
vaccinated. This suggests that barriers extend beyond awareness and likely include
access, offer rates, and health communication deficits.

Chunk 31:
The age-stratified analysis demonstrated that awareness was highest among younger
respondents and declined progressively across older age groups. This pattern may
reflect differences in educational exposure, recency of public health messaging, or
generational familiarity with vaccine-preventable disease campaigns. Importantly, despite low awareness and vaccination uptake, support for public health
intervention was extremely high.

Chunk 32:
This suggests that the major barrier in this sample
is not ideological resistance, but rather insufficient dissemination of information and
limited preventive outreach. 10

Chunk 33:
5. Conclusion
Among 100 respondents, awareness of HPV, awareness of HPV-associated cancers,
and HPV vaccination uptake were all found to be suboptimal. Knowledge was espe-
cially limited regarding HPV’s association with head and neck cancer and the broader
protective role of vaccination.

Chunk 34:
However, the very high support for NHS-funded vaccination and public awareness
campaigns represents a clear opportunity for policy-driven intervention. Structured ed-
ucational campaigns, improved communication strategies, and expanded institutional
outreach may help close the current awareness and vaccination gap. Data Transparency Statement
